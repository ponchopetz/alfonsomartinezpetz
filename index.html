Why sales, why now

Five years of phone sales taught me that the part most people dread is the part I'm built for: the objection, and the work of finding out what someone actually needs before I try to sell them anything. A patient recently called to skip his order over cost. By the end of the call he had re-enrolled, and told me it was worth it.

Over the past year I also learned to build software, including StatSnap, an NFL analytics app. The part I looked forward to was never the code. It was explaining it. Ask me what an API is and I'll tell you it's a restaurant: the front end is the menu, the back end is the kitchen, and you don't need to see the kitchen to get your food. Watching that click for someone who couldn't follow the technical version is the same rush as a good sales call.

Learning to build didn't make me want to write code all day. It showed me where I'm most useful: being the person who makes a technical product make sense to the people who have to buy it. Five years of consultative sales plus real technical fluency isn't a fallback. It's the profile a technical BDR seat is written for.
